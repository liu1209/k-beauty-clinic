With age, the two sides of your face drift apart.

Can Botox fix facial asymmetry? Often, yes. When one side of the face pulls harder or has dropped, carefully dosed Botox can relax the stronger side and lift the weaker one. At K-Beauty Clinic in Coquitlam, Dr. Yang treats facial asymmetry with Liftox, planning each side separately.

The two sides of the face don't age at the same speed. Differences you never noticed when you were younger often become clearly visible after 50. One eyebrow or corner of the mouth sits lower, or your smile starts to pull to one side.

In many patients, Dr. Yang also sees signs of small, earlier nerve or muscle changes that the person never noticed. This kind of imbalance builds up slowly over years, and Liftox at K-Beauty Clinic is designed to correct it.

Lasers, fillers and lifting devices treat both sides of the face in much the same way, so they can't correct a face where one side pulls harder or has dropped further.

With Liftox, Dr. Yang assesses each side separately and doses each side differently — relaxing the side that pulls harder and lifting the side that has dropped — to bring your face back into balance.

Facial Asymmetry: Your Questions, Answered

Why is my face becoming more asymmetrical with age?

The two sides age at different speeds, and small nerve or muscle changes from the past, or habits like chewing and sleeping on one side, add up over the years. It often shows most after 50.

Will Botox make one side of my face look stiff?

No. The dose on each side is set separately so both sides move naturally together.

Can fillers fix facial asymmetry?

Filler can even out volume, but it can't change how muscles pull. Most uneven faces need the muscles rebalanced first.

How long does Botox for facial asymmetry last?

Results look their best for about 3‒4 months and can last up to 6 months for some people.

What kinds of facial asymmetry can be treated without surgery?

Imbalance that has built up gradually over years — from ageing, earlier nerve or muscle changes, or habits. If your face has changed suddenly and recently, please see your family doctor first.
